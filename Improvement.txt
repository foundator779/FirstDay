FIRSTDAY: HACKATHON ASSESSMENT AND IMPROVEMENT PLAN
Assessment date: September 24, 2026
Event: Build, Ship, Shape: Amazon Developer Hackathon

RECOMMENDATION

Keep FirstDay and sharpen its main promise: help a learner catch a
misunderstanding before acting on it. After a training conversation, the learner
explains what they intend to do. FirstDay compares that explanation with the
source, surfaces a specific mismatch or missing context, helps clarify it, and
rehearses the confirmed action. Section 11 defines this stronger direction.
The optional evening correction conversation in section 10 supports this loop
by letting users repair the app's interpretation and speaker attribution.

Adults with ADHD learning a new job or procedure remain an audience hypothesis
to test. Use short sessions and reliable recovery after interruptions. The
proposed product direction is understanding-checking and targeted rehearsal;
the audience experiment does not require a general ADHD assistant or treatment
claim. These proposals do not change the current implementation or its score.

Current editorial score: 68/100, before any friction-log bonus.
Provisional competitive position: approximately 5th of the seven projects
considered here, with a plausible 3rd-6th range because evidence is uneven.
This is a comparison of documented product strength, NOT an official placing,
probability of winning, or ranking of the entire hackathon field.

More urgent than the score: FirstDay has not yet demonstrated the required
actual Bee-data path. Its current evidence is insufficient to establish a
Stage One pass. A polished fictional-data demo cannot resolve that gap.

The strongest route to contention is:
1. Prove actual Bee capture -> import -> rehearsal on the intended device.
2. Demonstrate one source-backed misunderstanding check and targeted retry.
3. Make Change Drill work from a second actual Bee conversation in the UI.
4. Make the app resume reliably and remove fragile demo assumptions.
5. Show observed user value against a simple transcript/notes baseline.
6. Deliver a clear, under-three-minute video and specific product feedback.

4. CONCRETE FIRSTDAY GAPS FOUND IN THE CODE

A. The visible Change Drill entry point is fixture-only.
   apps/mobile/src/practice-panel.tsx:33 chooses an update ID only when
   sourceKind is fixture, using SYNTHETIC_UPDATES. The recap update button then
   requests a fixture conversation. The compare/confirm backend exists, but
   the inspected screen does not expose equivalent live conversation selection.
   Improvement: let the learner choose a second processed Bee conversation,
   review/consent to it, and run the existing comparison and confirmation flow.
   Preserve evidence from both recordings. Do not call this automatic live
   synchronization until detection, reconciliation and UI delivery are proven.

B. Practice disappears unless exactly three instructions are confirmed.
   apps/mobile/src/practice-panel.tsx:86 returns null before practice when the
   count is not three. Real extraction or rejecting an uncertain card can yield
   another count. The user may have no obvious way to start or recover.
   Improvement: clearly guide selection of the supported number of confirmed
   cards. Only broaden counts after checking/updating the shared contract;
   do not simply remove the guard and assume the engine accepts every count.

C. The production API still creates a memory repository.
   services/api/src/index.ts:74 wires createMemoryRepository. Supabase schema
   and RLS work exists, but that does not establish durable runtime persistence.
   Improvement: finish DB-002, then demonstrate restart/reload recovery and
   confirm that revision/consent checks still hold across resumed sessions.

D. The convenient Bedrock launcher is not the live Bee launcher.
   services/api/src/demo-server.ts explicitly restricts its gateway to fixture
   data. A successful Nova Pro demonstration therefore proves real inference
   over fictional sources, not the required actual Bee source interaction.
   Improvement: document and verify the real bridge/API/client configuration,
   including a safe phone-to-API connection and valid learner authentication.
   Keep Bee credentials and the bridge bearer token local to the bridge/API.

E. Native speech and the keyboard repair still need physical-device evidence.
   Do not promise real voice quality based on controller tests or Appetize.
   Keep text as a supported answer path and show voice only after validation.

   The inspected practice screen advances through situations with feedback and
   retry. Market it as scenario rehearsal; do not imply a rich branching
   simulation, completed handoff sharing, or proven mastery without showing it.
   The existing recap's careful language is a strength worth preserving.

5. SHOULD FIRSTDAY TARGET PEOPLE WITH ADHD?

Yes as a focused audience experiment. No to a full pivot based only on comments.

The fit is plausible: NIMH lists difficulty remembering daily tasks, organizing
activities and following instructions among possible adult ADHD difficulties.
That supports investigating this problem, not claiming FirstDay treats ADHD or
that every person with ADHD needs the same product. [8]

The missing distinction is between remembering an instruction, understanding
it, starting the action and sustaining it. FirstDay mainly helps rehearse and
retrieve an instruction. If target users already know what to do but struggle
to start, adding quizzes may create more work without addressing their need.

A relevant HCI study reports interviews with 22 ADHD-identifying adults and a
concept study with 20 more, emphasizing social/emotional support around task
management. It is useful design research, not clinical evidence for FirstDay
or proof that a wearable reminder product will retain customers. [9]

Why not build a general ADHD memory assistant?
- Bee already promotes summaries, suggested to-dos, memories and reminders.
  Another generic memory/reminder layer needs a much sharper benefit. [10]
- "Helps ADHD" is an audience label, not a distinct job the product performs.
- A full assistant expands into schedules, task prioritization, notifications
  and integrations while leaving the real Bee demo requirement unresolved.
- It discards the strongest existing differentiator: practicing an instruction
  and unlearning an outdated version with visible source evidence.

Recommended positioning to test:
"FirstDay turns spoken training into short, source-backed rehearsals so you
can practise the next step and revisit it when the instructions change."

Initial audience: adults with ADHD starting a new role or learning a recurring,
low-risk procedure. Keep the product usable without disclosing a diagnosis.
Let research determine whether ADHD-specific positioning helps or narrows the
audience unnecessarily. Learning a new job is the initial use case; it does not
need a different company name or a wholesale rewrite.

Design hypotheses worth testing, in order:
1. One clear next action and a short, predictable session.
2. Reliable save/resume after leaving the app or being interrupted.
3. Optional listening/speaking plus equally usable text controls.
4. A plainly labeled way to revisit the last confirmed instruction.
5. Optional learner-chosen pre-shift rehearsal, only after persistence works.

Avoid mandatory streaks, punishment for missed practice, unsolicited reminders
and public performance comparisons. Treat this as usability design to validate,
not a claim that all ADHD users prefer the same interface. Keep practice private
and separate from employer performance scoring. Do not infer ADHD from speech,
introduce medication advice or claim symptom reduction.

What the video comments establish:
In Becca Farsace's "I spent 30 days with this controversial AI gadget," I verified
a comment suggesting ADHD support through reminders. It showed 12 likes when
read. Other visible comments raised privacy concerns and reported speaker
confusion. The page showed 454 comments; only a loaded subset (40 comment links)
was inspected, so I did not verify the total number of ADHD comments. [11]

This supports investigating reminder/recall needs, not yet demand for rehearsal.
These anecdotes do not establish present product defects, willingness to pay,
consent to workplace capture or sustained benefit. Test both the desired outcome
and recording acceptability. FirstDay's source review and human confirmation
are relevant safeguards; they cannot repair every upstream transcription error.

6. SMALL VALIDATION STUDY BEFORE COMMITTING TO THE POSITIONING

Recruit five consenting adults who identify as having ADHD and recently learned
a new work procedure, plus two trainers if available. Recruitment and interviews
are proposed work; no one was contacted for this assessment. Avoid requesting
medical records or making a diagnostic judgment.

Ask about their most recent concrete difficulty: what was explained, what broke
down afterward, what they tried, and whether the problem was recall, ambiguity,
starting, interruption or conflicting instructions. Ask about recording comfort
and whether their environment permits it.

Then compare a short transcript/checklist with FirstDay using matched low-risk
procedures. Alternate which approach comes first. Test a different application
scenario later rather than just repeating the same answer. Record:
- unaided task completion and setup/review time;
- correctly applied steps and source lookups;
- assistance and interruptions;
- delayed recall/application after 24-48 hours;
- preference with reasons and whether participants return voluntarily.

Proposed product decision thresholds, NOT existing results or clinical cutoffs:
- At least four of five can complete the core flow without coaching.
- At least three of five prefer rehearsal to notes for a concrete recurring use.
- At least three of five return for an offered follow-up without repeated nudges.
- No participant is misled into thinking an unconfirmed or outdated instruction
  is current. Investigate every observed unsupported grading result.

Report individual outcomes and denominators honestly. This convenience sample
can inform design; it cannot prove treatment efficacy, population-wide learning
improvement or product-market fit. If results are mixed, retain broader learner
positioning and describe ADHD as a research audience rather than a proven niche.

Pivot only if repeated interviews show that the current job is wrong:
- If recall/learning is the problem and rehearsal helps: keep FirstDay.
- If interruption recovery dominates and rehearsal adds burden: test an adjacent
  "resume the next confirmed step" mode using the same source-backed instructions.
- If users will not record training or cannot use Bee in the setting: choose a
  different consent-friendly learning context before changing the entire idea.
- If actual Bee access cannot be obtained: an ADHD rebrand does not solve track
  eligibility. Resolve device access or assess a genuinely qualifying alternative
  track, rather than relabeling fixture data.

10. REQUESTED ADDITION: AN EVENING CORRECTION CONVERSATION

Added September 24, 2026 following the user's request. This is a product
proposal and feasibility assessment, not an implemented feature or a revision
of the current API/state contract. It complements the proposed understanding
check: compare what was explained with the learner's interpretation, resolve
ambiguity, and focus subsequent practice on the confirmed instruction.

The experience:
An optional review of up to three important uncertainties from the day's
consented, selected conversations. The user can speak naturally to correct
attribution, transcription or interpretation, or skip anything they cannot
resolve. Aim for roughly two minutes; measure actual effort with users.

Example, proposed dialogue:
FirstDay: "The afternoon conversation suggests you agreed to set up the
display. Was that you or Sam?" [Show the relevant quote and time.]
User: "Sam said that. I was asking whether it was finished."
FirstDay: "For this conversation, I'll mark that statement as Sam's and remove
it from your proposed preparation. Is that accurate?"
User: "Yes."

The important behavior is the visible consequence: dependent instruction cards,
practice and questions are reviewed using the corrected attribution. Do not
silently assign work to Sam, message anyone, or change another person's account.
This example corrects FirstDay's interpretation, not a real-world task system.

Separate four correction types:
1. Speaker attribution: "That sentence was Sam, not me."
2. Transcription: "I said cannot, not can."
3. Interpretation: "That was a suggestion, not an instruction."
4. Actual change: "The trainer changed the rule this afternoon."
The first three dispute an earlier capture/interpretation. The fourth is a new
instruction with an effective context. Do not present every correction as a
policy change or erase history to make the versions appear consistent.

What "makes the app better" should mean:
- FirstDay retains the approved correction and applies it to later retrieval
  and generation that depends on that specific source.
- A user can reopen, revise or undo their correction.
- A corrected name in one conversation is not a permanent voice identity.
- Corrections do not by themselves retrain an AI model, improve Bee's acoustic
  recognition or prove accuracy on future recordings.

Bee feasibility, checked against official documentation:
- Pioneer documents hold-and-speak input for Bee chat or notes. The consumer
  app also describes owner voice training during onboarding. These do not
  establish a third-party API for voice retraining or custom chat routing. [12]
- Developer tools expose conversation/journal reads and voice-note-related
  journal events. Bee facts can be created, updated and confirmed. [13][14]
- No documented mechanism for FirstDay to rewrite speaker labels in original
  conversations, retrain voice identification, or take over Bee's built-in
  assistant was found in the reviewed references. The proxy endpoint catalog
  is explicitly non-exhaustive, so this is an unverified capability rather
  than a claim that Amazon could never support it. [13]

Practical delivery plan:
Start with the evening dialogue in FirstDay's iPhone app, using its voice/text
interaction. Separately verify a Pioneer correction-note path: speak a note,
retrieve its processed content through the local bridge, associate it with the
correct conversation/span, and review it in FirstDay before applying it. The
current FirstDay bridge has no journal import contract, so that path needs a
documented contract extension if corrections arrive as journals rather than
ordinary conversations. Do not promise that holding Pioneer's button directly
launches a FirstDay dialogue, or that FirstDay controls its response playback.

Keep correction prompts and outputs out of the training extractor unless the
learner explicitly selects them as new evidence. An app's spoken question can
itself be captured by Bee; it must not become independent corroboration of the
very claim being questioned. Test this feedback-loop risk on the real device.

Preserve evidence:
- Keep the imported source quote, revision and speaker label unchanged.
- Store the user's correction separately, with the affected source/span,
  correction time, confirming user and before/after meaning. This is a future
  data requirement, not a new schema or endpoint defined by this report.
- Display "user-corrected" meaning separately from the original transcript.
  Never manufacture a verbatim source quote from an edited interpretation.
- Keep disputed third-party policy out of grading until the conflict is
  resolved with adequate evidence. A user's recollection is not automatically
  authoritative evidence of what a trainer said.
- Withhold affected practice while under review; regenerate the relevant
  material after resolution. Preserve the history of earlier attempts.
- Handle source revocation and deletion for corrections and their derivatives
  as well as for the original import. Keep Bee credentials in the local bridge.

Optional later Bee write-back:
Offer a separate, explicit choice to update a particular Bee fact through its
supported commands. Keep the association with the exact fact and correction,
make repeat requests safe, and distinguish local success from upstream success.
Do not claim that updating a fact corrects its original transcript, trains the
speaker recognizer, or guarantees how Bee's own assistant will answer later.
Local FirstDay correction should remain useful without this integration.

Minimum demonstration and verification:
1. Import a real, consented Bee conversation and identify an attribution or
   interpretation issue. If an error is deliberately injected for testing,
   label it as such; do not claim Bee produced it naturally.
2. Complete a spoken correction, review its meaning and confirm it.
3. Show the original source alongside the correction and the resulting change
   to one affected instruction/practice item.
4. Reload and verify persistence; undo and verify consistent dependent state.
5. Verify no changes to similarly labeled speakers in other conversations,
   no changes without confirmation, and no use after source consent revocation.
6. Test misunderstood correction speech, two possible target conversations,
   contradictory corrections, interrupted review and repeated requests.

Product value and scope:
This adds a reason to return and a way to repair the app's understanding. It
also creates an immediate demo of user control. The stronger proposition is
capture -> clarify -> correct -> practise, rather than treating every captured
sentence as established truth. This is a hypothesis to test, not an automatic
increase to the existing idea score. Keep it restricted to relevant training
and preparation; avoid expanding the MVP into a general daily-life assistant.

Before implementation, claim a build task and update spec.md for correction
provenance, dispute handling, invalidation, persistence and any new Bee boundary.
DB-002 and physical voice/Bee verification remain relevant dependencies. This
proposal alone does not change their status or satisfy live-data eligibility.

11. STRONGER DIRECTION: CATCH MISUNDERSTANDINGS BEFORE ACTION

Added September 24, 2026. This expands the earlier idea discussion into a
concrete product proposal. It is not a claim that the feature is implemented,
unique in the market, or guaranteed to receive 5/5 from a judge.

Proposed pitch:
"FirstDay helps you catch what you misunderstood in a real conversation,
clarify it, and practise the right action before your next shift."

The central question is: "What will you do when this situation happens?"
The learner's own explanation gives FirstDay something concrete to compare
against the selected training evidence. The app must not infer understanding
from silence, attention, confidence, or simply having recorded a conversation.

Proposed core loop:
1. Capture: import a selected, consented Bee training conversation.
2. Explain: ask the learner to describe their intended action in their own words.
3. Compare: show the relevant source quote beside the learner's interpretation.
   Identify a specific possible mismatch, exception or missing condition.
4. Clarify: ask for missing context before judging. Let the learner correct a
   capture error, or save an unresolved question to ask the trainer. Unsupported
   or disputed instructions remain outside grading until adequately resolved.
5. Rehearse: generate one short situation that tests the confirmed distinction.
6. Revisit: use an optional evening review to repair remaining errors, and Change
   Drill when a later conversation genuinely changes the instruction.

Illustrative demo, using fictional content:
Trainer: "New reservations last three days. Reservations already made keep
their original seven-day window."
Learner: "Tomorrow I'll cancel anything that has been held for four days."
FirstDay: "Your plan could also cancel an existing seven-day reservation.
Does this reservation predate the change?" [Show both source clauses.]
After the relevant context is established, practise one older reservation and
one new reservation. If the date is unknown, preserve the uncertainty and ask
for it; do not label either action correct by guessing. This distinguishes a
learner's overgeneralization from an actual later change in the rule.

How the evening conversation fits:
The understanding check can uncover a learner misunderstanding; the evening
review can uncover a mistake made by the app, such as assigning a trainer's
sentence to the learner. Treat both as reviewable possibilities. Confirm the
correction, preserve the original source, and update only affected preparation.
The Pioneer integration limits and correction safeguards in section 10 apply.

Why this is a stronger idea candidate:
- It names a concrete problem and moment of value: a mistaken intended action
  is noticed while the learner still has time to clarify and rehearse it.
- The demonstration can show source, interpretation, mismatch and corrected
  response together, making the proposed benefit easy to inspect.
- It builds on FirstDay's existing source evidence, learner responses, private
  questions and Change Drill. The new comparison/review behavior still needs
  implementation and validation; existing pieces do not establish completion.
- For the competitor comparison, this is the proposed differentiating behavior
  to test. The earlier ranking does not prove that competitors lack it.

Smallest useful scope:
One learner, one training situation, one selected conversation, one explanation,
one possible mismatch and one focused rehearsal. An optional evening review
can cover up to three unresolved items as described in section 10. Keep a text
fallback and support the first dialogue in FirstDay while verifying Pioneer
input separately. A wholesale rebuild or a new product name is unnecessary.

What would support a higher idea assessment:
Observe whether learners have this problem and find the intervention useful.
Compare with reviewing the same transcript or notes: does the learner identify
and explain the relevant distinction on a new scenario? Record false mismatch
alerts, missed ambiguities, completion effort and correction usefulness. A
successful rehearsal is not proof of fewer real workplace mistakes. Keep the
current 4/5 idea estimate as the existing-product assessment until new evidence
supports reassessment; no concept alone guarantees a 5/5 or a hackathon win.

Implementation handoff:
This section updates the strategy recommendation. Sections 7 and 8 retain the
original delivery plan and demo outline; revise them around the validated
misunderstanding example when scheduling implementation. Claim build work and
update spec.md before introducing any new data, API or state contract. The
live-Bee eligibility, persistence and device-verification gaps still apply.
